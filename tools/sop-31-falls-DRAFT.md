## SOP 31: Falls prevention and response

Staff prevent falls at every visit, and respond to a fall by assessing before
moving: a client who cannot get up on their own with coaching waits on the
floor for paramedics, however long that takes.

**Core principles**

- **Stop, don't rush.** A fast lift off the floor causes more harm than a few
  minutes of assessment.
- **Never lift a client off the floor.** Not by hand, not with a co-worker.
  Atria has no floor lifts. If the client cannot get up themselves with
  coaching, call 911.
- **Don't catch a falling client.** Guide them down along your body and
  protect their head. Catching an adult injures you both.
- **Every fall is reported,** including falls with no visible injury, and
  falls the client asks you not to report.

**Prevention, every visit**

1. **Check the path.** Clear walkways, cords and clutter; wipe spills.
2. **Check the person.** Footwear, glasses, and the mobility aid's brakes,
   tips and height.
3. **Check the room.** Lighting, especially the route to the toilet at night,
   and the call device within reach.
4. **Check the plan.** The RN completes a falls risk assessment at intake and
   after every fall, and writes the fall plan into the care plan.

### Part A: a fall in the home

1. **Stop and make it safe.** Don't rush in. Look for water, broken glass, a
   stove left on, pets, or a walker tangled underneath. An injured worker
   cannot help anyone.
2. **Check responsiveness and breathing.** Say their name and tap their
   shoulder. Not responding, or not breathing normally: call 911, put the
   phone on speaker, and follow the dispatcher. Start CPR if trained. If there
   is a DNR Confirmation Form on file, tell 911 and follow it.
3. **Keep them still and ask.** Get down to their level. What happened —
   did they trip, or feel dizzy or black out first? Where does it hurt? Did
   they hit their head? How long have they been on the floor? Then look them
   over head to toe without moving them.
4. **Check for red flags.** Any one of these means they stay where they are:
   call 911, then your supervisor.

   | Red flag | What to look for |
   | --- | --- |
   | Head | Hit their head while on a blood thinner (apixaban, rivaroxaban, warfarin), even if they feel fine. Vomiting, drowsiness, severe headache, seizure, or unconscious at any point. New confusion, or no memory of the fall. |
   | Spine | Neck or back pain, or numbness, tingling or weakness in arms or legs. No pillow under the head. |
   | Fracture | One leg shorter or turned outward, hip or groin pain, a limb at an odd angle, cannot bear weight. Bone through skin, or bleeding that will not stop with firm pressure. |
   | The fall as a symptom | Chest pain, breathlessness, palpitations or fainting before the fall. Face droop, arm weakness or slurred speech — note the time it started. |
   | Long lie | On the floor over an hour, or an unknown time, especially if cold, weak or confused. |
   | Cannot get up | They cannot rise themselves with coaching. |

5. **Protect them while you wait.** Keep them still and warm, with a blanket
   over them and something between them and a cold floor. No pillow if the
   head, neck or back is involved. Press gently on bleeding; cover skin tears
   without pulling the flap off. Nothing to eat, drink or take by mouth — they
   may need surgery. Help with continence needs. Have the medication list
   ready; paramedics ask about blood thinners first. Never leave them alone on
   the floor.
6. **Decide on getting up — all three must be true.** No red flags and no new
   pain moving arms and legs. The client wants to get up and feels able. They
   can do it themselves with your coaching, or with a client-owned
   floor-recovery device you are trained on. Any "no": protect them and call
   911.
7. **Coach with words, not your back.** Roll onto their side, onto hands and
   knees, crawl to a sturdy chair, one foot flat, push up to sit. You steady
   the chair; you do not lift. Rest at least 10 minutes, then recheck pain,
   dizziness, confusion and breathing. Anything new: call 911.
8. **Report before you leave.** Call the supervisor for every fall, even with
   no injury, and even if the client objects.
9. **Document the same shift.** Incident report, facts only: "found on the
   floor beside the bed", not "fell out of bed", unless someone saw it.

### Part B: a fall outside the home

On a walk, at an appointment, in a store or a car park, the procedure is the
same — Part A, steps 1 to 9, still applies. What changes is that you do not
control the ground, the weather, the traffic or the crowd, and you have no
equipment. So you call for help sooner.

1. **Danger first.** Check for cars, ice, stairs and crowds before you go to
   them. Move a client only if staying put is dangerous, such as in moving
   traffic, and only as far as needed.
2. **Call 911 earlier than you would indoors.** Hard, cold or wet ground and
   no chair or bed to work with mean a long lie becomes dangerous much faster
   outside. If in doubt, call.
3. **Give the exact location.** Address or nearest intersection, store name,
   which entrance, which parking level. Ask someone to meet the ambulance.
4. **Keep them warm and dry.** Coats or blankets over and around them; ask
   store staff for blankets. In summer heat, get them into shade.
5. **Stop well-meaning helpers.** Strangers will try to pull the client up.
   Thank them and give them another job: *"Thank you, please don't lift her.
   Paramedics are coming. Could you watch for the ambulance?"*
6. **Never leave them to get help.** Call from where you are. Ask nearby staff
   for a first aid kit or an AED.
7. **If they insist they are fine,** you cannot force care. Explain your
   concern, stay with them, get them home safely if there are no red flags,
   and report it like any other fall.

**Before every outing:** phone charged; the client's medication list,
allergies and emergency contacts with you; walker or chair checked; route and
weather thought through for ice, curbs and stairs.

### After any fall: watch for 24 to 72 hours

At every visit after a fall, report straight away: new or worse pain,
headache, vomiting, drowsiness or new confusion, new trouble walking or
bearing weight, new bruising or swelling. Brain bleeds and fractures can
appear hours or days later.

**Documentation:** incident report the same shift, post-fall monitoring notes
at each visit for 72 hours, and an updated falls risk assessment and fall plan
by the RN.
